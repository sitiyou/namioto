# Backing vocals and sung responses

By default, time the supplied lyrics and skip searching the recording for unprinted backing vocals. Discovery takes time and short calls are easy to misidentify. When the supplied lyrics already include responses or mark another voice, identify the singer and timing for those passages as part of normal alignment. Search for additional parts only on request, starting at the user's timecode when given.

Compare the phrase in the original mix with a backing-vocal stem if available. For stereo audio, the difference between left and right channels can bring out a panned response. Include the lead-in, then isolate the suspected answer and compare its attack and ending with the lead. A second voice may repeat exactly the printed word, enter on its final syllable, or continue underneath the next line. When a short call sounds like an interjection, compare its vowel and duration with the word the lead is holding. A backing voice may be echoing that exact word. Keep its spelling from the lyric source when the reading matches. A loud side signal alone can also come from reverb or instruments. Use [Qwen3-ASR for unclear words](sung-words.md#identify-a-backing-response) when helpful. Record the time, overlap and likely wording separately. Keep uncertain syllables as review notes until resolved.

Identify recurring response patterns across verses and choruses. Prefer inline for short calls and echoes, including a brief overlap with a sustained lead syllable. When both placements read well, reuse the placement established for the same musical role, even if the words change. In either layout, give the backing part its own timed notes, lyric channel. Preserve each voice's performed onset and release, including overlap.

- **Inline parentheses** suit a short echo or call that reads naturally next to the word it answers and fits comfortably on the row. A backing voice can enter while the lead is still holding that word. Place its channel wrapper immediately after the complete word or phrase it answers, before any later lead words. Check the backing onset against the lead word's sung interval. A response belongs at the end of the row when it answers the final phrase. Keep each span’s own note clock for the external template. ASCII parentheses are KRC grouping syntax. For Japanese lyrics, use visible punctuation such as `（ ）` and verify the brackets consume no sung notes.
- **A separate upper track** is appropriate for a particularly long response or sustained overlap in which the singer needs to follow two independent phrases. For a crowded short response, first try a natural break in the host lyric and check it at singing speed. Put the backing lyrics on their own display line and record their channel and harmony role for the external template.

Whenever a response is added or revised, manually recheck the timing of both voices at each affected occurrence. Include the last clear lead syllable, the response and the next attack. With audio input, loop the handoff and listen for the lead release and response onset and release. With visual or numeric input, inspect the same handoff on the source audio clock. Run `uv run /path/to/skill/scripts/inspect_audio.py vocal.wav START END --png handoff.png --mark SECONDS=label` with seconds from the recording; account for any subtitle offset when marking note boundaries. The [inspection tool](../scripts/inspect_audio.py) prints short-window RMS in dBFS (`--window-ms`) and plots a shared-scale spectrogram of the original and stereo Mid/Side signals. Compare a clear sample of each voice, then follow its harmonics and vowel spectrum into the uncertain interval. A change in stereo energy helps locate a transition; overlapping voices, reverb and panning can produce similar measurements. Use GAME notes as additional evidence. When the release can only be bounded, record the interval and uncertainty for review. Adjust the affected channels’ source note boundaries and KRC note counts as needed, preserving audible gaps and genuine overlap.

Preview the handoff at singing speed. Once the lead falls silent its highlight should have completed, even if the row stays visible and the response continues. During a genuine overlap, verify that each highlight advances on its own words. For an upper track, also check clearance around ruby and count-ins.
