# GAME and alignment controls

The IPC methods `analysis.transcribe`, `analysis.align` and `analysis.clip` default to
`device=auto`, which uses the GPU backend selected in Settings → Devices if available and falls
back to CPU. Pass `device=cpu` to force CPU.

## Measure and preview

1. Measure BPM on the accompaniment or mix with `analysis.detect_bpm algorithm=wavetone`. Poll `analysis.bpm_result job=ID` and compare beats near the beginning, middle and end. Check half or double tempo and any growing displacement before moving notes.
2. Run GAME on the aligned vocal at the confirmed tempo. The editor keeps the raw inference result when it inserts quantized notes. Through IPC, set the tempo with `transport.set_bpm`, run `analysis.transcribe`, and get the raw attacks, releases and pitches with `analysis.transcription job=ID`.
3. In the GAME dialog, use **Quantize → 1/8** and **Estimate phase**. To try a different offset, adjust **Grid offset (ms)** in the editor and rerun with **Estimate phase** cleared. GAME can reuse the same raw result. Through IPC, preview with `analysis.transcription_grid job=ID cells=2 estimate_phase=true`, use `transport.set_grid_offset ms=VALUE` to set the offset, and call `analysis.transcription_grid job=ID cells=2` to preview it. Setting the offset moves the grid. The IPC preview returns proposed note times for a separate edit. [Grid fit check](timing-from-audio.md#check-the-eighth-note-grid)

## Resolve an uncertain phrase

Set the reviewed KRC with `lyrics.set`, run `analysis.align model=mms channel=CHANNEL`, and poll `analysis.alignment job=ID`. Once the grid is confirmed in the editor, use `analysis.phrase line=INDEX channel=CHANNEL estimate_phase=false` for every line. Compare current NOTE ownership, raw and snapped GAME, and the aligner's onsets, scores and bounds. Check `missing` and read the two kinds of boundary evidence together.

- `game.quantization` marks the onset and release separately. Review boundaries marked `review`, and check dropped notes, pushed onsets and closed gaps. `shift_ms` shows how far the boundary moved, with negative values indicating an earlier time. A small `midpoint_distance_ms` means a small change to the raw time could move the snap by a whole grid cell. Use `finer_grid` to test whether an identified event or rest survives a finer subdivision. [Grid fit check](timing-from-audio.md#check-the-eighth-note-grid)
- `signal_summary` is included by default for positioned notes. Compare the harmonic level at the NOTE pitch and the short-window RMS `before`, `between` and `after` the raw and snapped boundaries. At a release, `harmonic_drop` gives an approximate time for a clear energy drop. Check these measurements even for unflagged snaps when the vowel or rest disagrees with GAME.
- `pitch_change_before` uses the editor's spectrum to compare the two candidate NOTE pitches across a close handoff. A negative `before_db` and positive `after_db` support the pitch change. Use the recording to place the boundary.

| Check | What to compare |
| --- | --- |
| Entrance and line handoff | Find the last clear sound of the preceding phrase and the first clear attack of this one. Check for a missing pickup, a syllable on the previous line's tail, or several MMS onsets compressed near a rest. If the alignment moved to a repeat or another voice, set explicit `start` and `end` on `analysis.phrase` around the actual phrase and recheck with the surrounding sounds. |
| NOTE attacks and pitch | Compare raw and snapped GAME at weak attacks, short adjacent notes, same-pitch boundaries and isolated pitch jumps. Confirm a missed pickup, reattack or pitch step against the beat and vocal evidence before adding a note. Follow harmonic groups for stepped pitch changes. Slides, vibrato and voice bleed can produce short false notes. |
| Release and breath | Use GAME note-offs, harmonic decay and short-window RMS to locate the tail, then compare the eighth-note version with the phrase around it. A harmonic drop locates an acoustic release. Use the grid fit check to decide whether that release changes the rhythm into the next attack or rest. MMS token ends describe the CTC alignment. |
| Sound ownership | Start with the musical NOTE stream. Give a sound its own NOTE when it occupies a distinct rhythmic or melodic event, including a same-pitch attack or a clearly placed unvoiced sound. Namioto divides a shared NOTE equally among its sounds. Check whether those divisions fit the sung entries, especially for a quick sound after a held vowel or at the start of the next word. If sharing starts the short sound too early or gives it too much time, check whether it has an independent rhythmic placement. Use the coarsest subdivision that preserves a distinct event, or use `.0` for a passing sound that needs no slot. A sustained sound can own several distinct pitch steps. Check that the next sound still consumes its intended NOTE. |
| Local correction | If a mostly syllabic line alternates between shared groups, zero slots and multi-NOTE sounds, check for an extra or missing NOTE at the first displacement. Compare the nearest clear sounds on both sides and the first sound of the following line. Use another occurrence of the same musical figure as a reference and check audible variations before adopting its mapping. |

Check both the vocal and mix when available. When native audio input is available, use MMS-vs-Yohane onset disagreement (e.g. `> 80 ms`), low alignment scores, short-window RMS drops and overlapping voice handoffs as a triage filter: listen directly to targeted 3–5 s clips — slowed to `0.65×–0.7×` for dense runs, subtle elisions (`.0`) or rapid call-and-response — instead of stepping blindly through every well-agreed phrase with `spectrum=true` or spectrogram PNGs. When working without audio input (or when listening still leaves a pitch or release ambiguous), request `spectrum=true` or `rms=true` for the disputed window, or use the [audio inspection script](../scripts/inspect_audio.py). Use individual harmonics, energy and listening together. A voiced pitch can be clearer in upper harmonics, and RMS also responds to consonants, breaths and accompaniment bleed.

Use [independent clip analysis](../../../docs/cli.md#inspect-an-audio-clip) when the whole-song result is ambiguous. Keep the complete disputed words and neighboring sounds in the window. Compare MMS and Yohane on the same KRC and audio if available. A low score, wide onset interval or cluster of nearly simultaneous onsets is a review cue. A narrow interval or agreement between models can still reflect the wrong lyric position. If the readings are in doubt, [verify what was sung](sung-words.md#check-lyric-readings) before editing notes. GAME can also recheck the bounded passage on a filtered or separated vocal on the same clock.

Keep display punctuation and symbols such as ☆, ♥ and ♪, then check ownership of the next sound. Use the [phrase preview](../../../docs/cli.md#review-and-edit-one-phrase) to confirm a local edit and its neighbors before applying it. If reviewing a wipe jump or stall at playback time, account for the known delivery offset to find it on the recording clock.

## Place a sound in an internal pause

When a `.0` sound falls between separated NOTEs in a continuous phrase, assign it a timing NOTE in the interval. Zero slots leave the sound's placement ambiguous between the preceding release and the next attack.

Locate the sound using the vocal and mix, the beat, and repeated phrases. Check whether it has a vowel, a weak or unvoiced consonant, or a closure. Use the local spectrum, RMS and alignment bounds together with listening. In `signal_summary.unmapped_gaps`, compare the high-frequency peak time and level with the gap's median to find possible friction or breath. An absent GAME note or an uncertain MMS onset can accompany a real but weak articulation.

Use the whole interval when it fits the articulation, such as a closure spanning the gap. If the sound occupies only part of the interval, use the coarsest subdivision that places it there and preserves the remaining rest. For example, a weak `し` just before `て` can take the last sixteenth of an eighth-note gap. Keep the neighboring release and attack where the recording places them. If the evidence leaves the position uncertain, choose the best-supported interval and record the uncertainty.
